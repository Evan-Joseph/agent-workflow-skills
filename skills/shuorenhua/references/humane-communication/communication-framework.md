# Communication Framework

Use this reference when the task needs strategy, not just nicer wording.

## Four Checks Before Speaking

1. Receiver: What does the other person need to decide, protect, or avoid?
2. Facts: What can be stated without exaggeration or guessing motive?
3. Relationship: Is the goal to open, maintain, repair, decline, or escalate?
4. Action: What is the one concrete next step that makes the message useful?

If any answer is missing, ask one concise clarification or proceed with an explicit assumption.

## NVC Without Formula Voice

Nonviolent communication is useful when tension, apology, complaint, gratitude, or conflict is involved. Use the four components as an internal check:

- Observation: concrete event, time, behavior, or message.
- Feeling: the sender’s state, not an accusation disguised as a feeling.
- Need: the underlying need such as clarity, fairness, safety, progress, time, respect, or direction.
- Request: positive, concrete, doable, and answerable now.

Do not mechanically write every message as “when I observe..., I feel...”. In Chinese professional messages, compress the structure into natural prose.

Gratitude should name what the person did, what need it helped, and why it mattered. Avoid judge-like praise such as “you are amazing” unless the relationship already supports that tone.

## Smart Questions

A strong question lowers the receiver’s effort:

- show that the sender has read, searched, tried, or prepared
- narrow the scope to 1-3 answerable questions
- include relevant facts and constraints
- keep the message short enough that the request is visible in one screen
- use a subject/title that identifies the object and the request

Do not ask the receiver to solve an undefined life problem, reconstruct missing context, or make a decision that belongs to the sender.

## Principled Negotiation

Use for coordination, offers, refusal, conflict, and trade-off discussion.

- Separate people from the problem: respect the person, name the issue.
- Focus on interests: explain why something matters, not just a fixed position.
- Offer options: give acceptable paths, not a single demand.
- Use criteria: refer to schedule, requirements, capacity, policy, evidence, or shared goals.

The default posture is firm on the substance and soft on the person. Do not bargain by guilt, threat, or false urgency.

## Influence Boundaries

Useful influence is ethical when it makes the receiver’s decision easier and more informed:

- give a reason for the request
- make the request specific
- give the receiver real choice
- acknowledge constraints without pretending to know their inner state
- thank concrete help or attention

Do not use reciprocity debt, artificial scarcity, social pressure, forced intimacy, or “psychological tricks” to push someone into agreement.

## Story Framing

For self-introduction, applications, project pitches, and mentor contact, use a compact story:

1. current state: who the sender is and why this context exists
2. concrete work: what they actually did, with verifiable details
3. connection: why this receiver, topic, or opportunity fits
4. request: what the receiver can do next

Specific detail beats self-labeling. Let facts show competence; do not write “I am excellent, hardworking, and passionate” as the main evidence.
