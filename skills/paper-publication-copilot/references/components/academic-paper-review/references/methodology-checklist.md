# Methodology Checklist

Use this reference for methodology-focus review, empirical-paper review, and method-heavy re-review.

## Universal Checks

- Research question is answerable by the selected design.
- Unit of analysis matches the claim.
- Sampling, inclusion, exclusion, and recruitment criteria are explicit.
- Measures or variables are operationalized, not only named.
- Analysis method matches data type and assumptions.
- Limitations name threats that actually affect the study.
- Claims do not exceed design strength.

## Quantitative Studies

- Power, sample size, missing data, exclusions, and outlier handling are reported.
- Model assumptions are checked or justified.
- Effect sizes and confidence intervals appear alongside p-values when relevant.
- Multiple-comparison handling is stated.
- Robustness, sensitivity, subgroup, or ablation analyses match the claim.
- Randomization, matching, controls, or causal identification are sufficient for causal language.
- Tables and figures expose denominators, uncertainty, and units.

## Qualitative Studies

- Case, participant, or corpus selection is justified.
- Coding process, coder training, disagreement handling, and audit trail are described.
- Saturation, reflexivity, triangulation, or negative cases are addressed where appropriate.
- Claims are supported by excerpts, observations, or document evidence.
- The analysis distinguishes participant claims from researcher interpretation.

## Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

- Review question is PICOS/SPIDER-like and eligibility criteria are pre-specified.
- Search covers at least two appropriate databases or explains why not.
- Screening, extraction, and exclusion reasons are reproducible.
- Risk-of-bias tool fits the included study designs.
- Heterogeneity is assessed before pooling.
- Narrative synthesis avoids vote counting.
- Certainty of evidence is separated from effect direction.

## Red Flags

- Causal claims from cross-sectional or convenience-sample evidence.
- Unsupported generalization from one case, one platform, or one institution.
- Missing denominator or unclear population.
- Selective reporting: only significant outcomes appear.
- New experiments or analyses claimed in response letters but absent from the manuscript.
- Citations used as method evidence when the cited source only provides background.
